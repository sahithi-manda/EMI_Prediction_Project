## Slide 2: Problem Statement & Motivation
* **The Problem**: 
  * Defaults and bad debts arise due to high debt-to-income ratios and subprime borrowing.
  * Traditional credit rating methods focus only on credit approval status (yes/no), ignoring actual disposable cash capacity.
  * Over-leveraged borrowers suffer financial stress, while banks face loan loss provisions.
* **The Goal**: Build a unified ML pipeline that performs:
  1. **Eligibility Prediction**: Classify if the borrower meets credit guidelines.
  2. **Affordability Capacity Estimation**: Predict the maximum monthly repayment constraint (EMI) they can comfortably pay.

### Presenter Notes:
> "Credit rating agencies approve loans based on credit scores, but they often fail to look at how much a borrower can actually pay monthly. Our motivation is to develop a dual-engine application. First, it identifies if a user matches banking credit norms. Second, it guides them on what maximum EMI is safe for their monthly cash flow, preventing default before a loan is even sanctioned."

## Slide 3: Proposed System Architecture
* **Diagram/Flow**:
  * Input Form -> Data Preprocessing -> Pipeline Columns -> Models -> Streamlit Frontend.
* **Core Components**:
  * **Synthetic Dataset**: 2,500 customer records incorporating age, monthly income, monthly expenses, credit scores, existing debts, and dependents.
  * **Preprocessing Pipeline**: One-Hot Encoder for categorical inputs, StandardScaler for numerical inputs. All packaged in an `sklearn.compose.ColumnTransformer`.
  * **Dual Models**: Separate classification and regression engines.

### Presenter Notes:
> "This slide illustrates the flow of our system. Data enters from a frontend form. It passes through our custom scikit-learn ColumnTransformer, which performs scaling and encoding. This preprocessed data is then fed into two models: a classification model to determine eligibility, and a regression model to estimate the maximum affordable EMI. Finally, the results are loaded into a real-time Streamlit dashboard."

## Slide 4: Classification Engine - Eligibility Prediction
* **Objective**: Predict a binary target `EMI_Eligibility` (0: Ineligible, 1: Eligible).
* **Models Evaluated**:
  * Logistic Regression (Accuracy: ~89.8%, Precision: ~86.6%, Recall: ~64.0%, F1-Score: ~73.6%)
  * Random Forest Classifier (Accuracy: ~88.4%, Precision: ~90.8%, Recall: ~53.2%, F1-Score: ~67.1%)
* **Model Selection**: **Logistic Regression** (higher overall F1-Score and balanced recall).
* **Key Visuals**: Confusion Matrix Heatmap.

### Presenter Notes:
> "For the eligibility task, we benchmarked Logistic Regression against Random Forest Classifier. In this case, Logistic Regression scored a higher F1-score of 73.6% compared to 67.1% for Random Forest. This indicates that the decision boundary generated by our credit risk logic maps well to the sigmoid function. Logistic Regression was serialized as our best classifier."

---

## Slide 5: Regression Engine - Affordability Estimation
* **Objective**: Predict a continuous target `Max_Affordable_EMI` (in INR).
* **Models Evaluated**:
  * Linear Regression ($R^2$ Score: ~71.4%, RMSE: ~8,757 INR)
  * Random Forest Regressor ($R^2$ Score: ~81.9%, RMSE: ~6,968 INR)
* **Model Selection**: **Random Forest Regressor** (highest $R^2$ score and lowest MAE of ~3,570 INR).
* **Key Visuals**: Residual plot showing variance.

### Presenter Notes:
> "To predict the continuous maximum affordable EMI, we trained a Linear Regression model and a Random Forest Regressor. The Random Forest Regressor outperformed Linear Regression with an R-squared score of 81.9% and a Root Mean Squared Error of approximately 6,968 INR. The model learns non-linear relationships, such as how credit score caps and dependent counts restrict disposable surplus. Random Forest was selected as our best regressor."

  * Real-time inference showing predicted eligibility, confidence rates, and risk profiles.
  * Affordability engine with a dynamic financial health score progress bar.
  * Built-in Amortization Tool simulating loan capacity based on predicted affordable EMI.
  * Interactive analytics (demographics distributions, heatmaps, and model feature importances).

### Presenter Notes:
> "We deployed the models in a local Streamlit web application. We prioritized UI aesthetics by injecting glassmorphism cards and custom CSS styling. A key feature is the state sharing: if a user types their income on the eligibility page, it is saved when they navigate to the affordability calculator. We also added an interactive loan capacity simulator to show how monthly EMI translates into a maximum principal loan amount."
